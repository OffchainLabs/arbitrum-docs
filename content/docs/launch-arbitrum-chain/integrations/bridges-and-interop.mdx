Your <Term id="arbitrum-chain">Arbitrum chain</Term> accepts deposits of its gas token from the moment you deploy it. The gas token is ETH or a <Term id="custom-gas-token">custom gas token</Term>. Users deposit it through the Inbox contract of your Arbitrum chain. For ERC-20 tokens, and for chains other than your <Term id="parent-chain">parent chain</Term>, you set up a bridge. The Arbitrum stack has no protocol-level token-bridging standard. Instead, you choose one or more of the options on this page, and you can combine them.

Each option has a different owner. You deploy some options yourself. A token issuer, Circle, or a bridge provider deploys the others. The tables on this page show who acts for each option.

## Which option fits

What do you want your Arbitrum chain to do:

- **Move ERC-20 tokens between the parent chain and your Arbitrum chain.** Deploy the [canonical token bridge](/launch-arbitrum-chain/deploy/token-bridge).
- **Earn yield on the tokens that the canonical token bridge holds.** Evaluate a [Yield Bridge](/launch-arbitrum-chain/deploy/yield-bridge), a variant of the canonical token bridge.
- **Offer USDC.** Deploy [bridged USDC](/launch-arbitrum-chain/integrations/bridged-usdc), so that Circle can later upgrade it to native USDC.
- **Use an interop token as your gas token.** Configure a [native mint/burn gas token](/launch-arbitrum-chain/chain-config/costs/configure-native-mint-burn), such as a LayerZero omnichain fungible token (OFT), an xERC20 token, or native USDC.
- **Reach chains other than your parent chain.** Work with a [third-party bridge](#third-party-bridges).
- **Let users bridge from a web app.** [Add your Arbitrum chain to the Arbitrum bridge](/launch-arbitrum-chain/integrations/bridge-ui).

## Lock-and-mint and burn-and-mint

The options use one of two models to move a token.

![Two ways a token reaches your Arbitrum chain. Left, lock-and-mint with the canonical token bridge: a gateway on the parent chain locks the token, and a gateway on your Arbitrum chain mints a copy. Deposits and withdrawals move the token only between the parent chain and your Arbitrum chain. Right, burn-and-mint with LayerZero OFT, xERC20 or native USDC: the token issuer's bridge connects the parent chain, your Arbitrum chain and other chains. The bridge burns the token on the source chain and mints it on the destination chain.](/img/arb-chain-bridge-token-models.svg)

- **Lock-and-mint.** The canonical token bridge locks the token in a <Term id="token-gateway">token gateway</Term> on the parent chain, and mints a copy on your Arbitrum chain. The copy moves only between your Arbitrum chain and the parent chain. The bridge uses your Arbitrum chain's own parent-to-child and child-to-parent messages, so it adds no third-party trust.
- **Burn-and-mint.** The token issuer allows one or more bridges to burn the token on one chain and mint it on another. The token is the same on every chain, so it can reach any chain that the issuer's bridge connects. Your Arbitrum chain then also trusts that bridge.

## Compare the options

| Option                                                                                             | What moves                                                                                      | Model                                                  | Who deploys it                                                                                                          | Requirements                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [Canonical token bridge](/launch-arbitrum-chain/deploy/token-bridge)                               | ERC-20 tokens, including wrapped ether (WETH), between the parent chain and your Arbitrum chain | Lock-and-mint, over your Arbitrum chain's own messages | You, with the Chain SDK, after your Arbitrum chain and a node run                                                       | A running chain                                                                                                                                                                                |
| [Yield Bridge](/launch-arbitrum-chain/deploy/yield-bridge)                                         | ERC-20 tokens; withdrawals return vault shares                                                  | Lock-and-mint; the locked tokens earn yield in a vault | You, with hands-on support that you request in the Arbitrum Discord                                                     | New token bridges only; you or a curator manage the vault                                                                                                                                      |
| [Bridged USDC](/launch-arbitrum-chain/integrations/bridged-usdc)                                   | USDC                                                                                            | Lock-and-mint, upgradeable to native USDC              | You deploy bridged USDC; Circle and you must both agree to move to native USDC                                          | Any Arbitrum chain; deploy it at launch, so that users never hold a second USDC that you must migrate; Circle's bridged USDC standard                                                          |
| [Native mint/burn gas token](/launch-arbitrum-chain/chain-config/costs/configure-native-mint-burn) | Your Arbitrum chain's gas token: a LayerZero OFT, an xERC20 token, or native USDC               | Burn-and-mint through the token issuer's bridge        | You deploy the bridge adapter, for example a LayerZero OFT; the <Term id="chain-owner">chain owner</Term> allowlists it | ArbOS 51 or later; `nitro-contracts` v3.1.0 and Nitro v3.9.3 or later; enable it at genesis with a [custom genesis](/launch-arbitrum-chain/deploy/custom-genesis-state), or on a running chain |
| [Third-party bridges](#third-party-bridges)                                                        | Tokens and messages, to chains beyond the parent chain                                          | Depends on the bridge provider                         | The bridge provider, by its own process                                                                                 | Set by each bridge provider                                                                                                                                                                    |

<Callout type="warn" title="A third-party bridge adds trust">

If a third-party bridge mints your Arbitrum chain's gas token, the value of that token depends on the bridge. A bug or a compromised key in the bridge adapter can mint gas tokens with no matching burn on the source chain. Whoever controls the adapter can sell those tokens on your Arbitrum chain or bridge them out, which takes value from the other holders. Your Arbitrum chain's liveness also depends on the bridge. By default, your Arbitrum chain issues its gas token only for deposits through its own Inbox and Bridge contracts. See [Use native interop token with mint/burn as your Arbitrum chain gas token](/launch-arbitrum-chain/chain-config/costs/configure-native-mint-burn) for the security checklist.

</Callout>

## Third-party bridges

A third-party bridge works on your Arbitrum chain only after the bridge provider adds support for it. Each bridge provider decides whether and how it supports a new chain. Contact each bridge provider to learn its current process. Offchain Labs does not endorse or rank these bridge providers.

| Provider                                           | How it works                                                                                                                                  | How your Arbitrum chain gets support                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| -------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [LayerZero](/third-party-docs/LayerZero/layerzero) | Messaging protocol. OFTs burn on one chain and mint on another.                                                                               | Contact LayerZero to ask for support for your Arbitrum chain. See the [LayerZero documentation](https://docs.layerzero.network/).                                                                                                                       |
| Hyperlane                                          | Messaging protocol. Warp Routes move tokens between chains.                                                                                   | Anyone can deploy the Hyperlane core contracts to an Ethereum Virtual Machine (EVM) chain with the Hyperlane CLI. See [Deploy to a new chain](https://docs.hyperlane.xyz/docs/guides/chains/deploy-hyperlane).                                          |
| Wormhole                                           | Messaging protocol verified by a network of Guardians. Native Token Transfers (NTT) move a token in burn-and-mint or hub-and-spoke mode.      | Wormhole Guardians must observe your Arbitrum chain, so Wormhole must add your Arbitrum chain to the chains it connects. See [Native Token Transfers](https://wormhole.com/docs/products/token-transfers/native-token-transfers/guides/deploy-to-evm/). |
| Across                                             | Intent-based bridge. Relayers fill a transfer on the destination chain, and Across repays them later.                                         | Across sets its own requirements for a new chain. Contact Across. See the [Across documentation](https://docs.across.to/).                                                                                                                              |
| Everclear (formerly Connext)                       | Settles intents across chains. It nets intents that move in opposite directions, settles xERC20 transfers, and sends messages with Hyperlane. | See the [Everclear documentation](https://docs.everclear.org/).                                                                                                                                                                                         |

xERC20 ([ERC-7281](https://github.com/connext/ERCs/blob/ERC-7281/ERCS/erc-7281.md)) is a draft token standard, not a bridge. The token issuer allows each bridge to mint and burn the token, and sets a rate limit for each bridge.

For more projects that support Arbitrum chains, see the [Arbitrum ecosystem portal](https://portal.arbitrum.io/chains/ecosystem).
