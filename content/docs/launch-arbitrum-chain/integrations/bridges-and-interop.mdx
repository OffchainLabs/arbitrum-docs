Your <Term id="arbitrum-chain">Arbitrum chain</Term> has no token bridge until you set one up. The Arbitrum stack has no token bridging standard at the protocol level. Instead, you choose one or more of the options on this page, and you can combine them.

Each option has a different owner. You deploy some options yourself. A token issuer, Circle, or a bridge provider deploys the others. The tables on this page show who acts for each option.

## Which option fits

Start from what you want your chain to do:

- **Move ETH and ERC-20 tokens between the parent chain and your chain.** Deploy the [canonical token bridge](/launch-arbitrum-chain/deploy/token-bridge). Most chains start here.
- **Earn yield on the tokens that the canonical bridge holds.** Evaluate a [Yield Bridge](/launch-arbitrum-chain/deploy/yield-bridge), a variant of the canonical token bridge.
- **Offer USDC.** Deploy [bridged USDC](/launch-arbitrum-chain/integrations/bridged-usdc), so that Circle can later upgrade it to native USDC.
- **Use an interop token as your gas token.** Configure a [native mint/burn gas token](/launch-arbitrum-chain/chain-config/costs/configure-native-mint-burn), such as a LayerZero OFT, an xERC20 token, or native USDC.
- **Reach chains other than your parent chain.** Work with a [third-party bridge](#third-party-bridges).
- **Let users bridge from a web app.** [Add your chain to the Arbitrum bridge](/launch-arbitrum-chain/integrations/bridge-ui).

## Lock-and-mint and burn-and-mint

The options use one of two models to move a token.

![Two ways a token reaches your Arbitrum chain. Left, lock-and-mint with the canonical token bridge: a gateway on the parent chain locks the token, and a gateway on your Arbitrum chain mints a copy. Deposits and withdrawals move the token only between the parent chain and your chain. Right, burn-and-mint with LayerZero OFT, xERC20 or native USDC: the token issuer's bridge connects the parent chain, your Arbitrum chain and other chains. The bridge burns the token on the source chain and mints it on the destination chain.](/img/arb-chain-bridge-token-models.svg)

- **Lock-and-mint.** The canonical token bridge locks the token in a <Term id="token-gateway">token gateway</Term> on the <Term id="parent-chain">parent chain</Term>, and mints a copy on your chain. The copy moves only between your chain and the parent chain. The bridge inherits the security of the parent chain.
- **Burn-and-mint.** The token issuer allows one or more bridges to burn the token on one chain and mint it on another. The token is the same on every chain, so it can reach any chain that the issuer's bridge connects. Your chain then also trusts that bridge.

## Compare the options

| Option                                                                                             | What moves                                                               | Model                                                  | Who deploys it                                                                                                | Requirements                                                         |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [Canonical token bridge](/launch-arbitrum-chain/deploy/token-bridge)                               | ETH and ERC-20 tokens, between the parent chain and your chain           | Lock-and-mint, secured by the parent chain             | You, with the Chain SDK, after your chain and a node run                                                      | A running chain                                                      |
| [Yield Bridge](/launch-arbitrum-chain/deploy/yield-bridge)                                         | Same as the canonical token bridge                                       | Lock-and-mint; the locked tokens earn yield in a vault | You, with hands-on support from Offchain Labs                                                                 | New token bridges only; you or a curator manage the vault            |
| [Bridged USDC](/launch-arbitrum-chain/integrations/bridged-usdc)                                   | USDC                                                                     | Lock-and-mint, upgradeable to native USDC              | You deploy bridged USDC; Circle and you must both agree to move to native USDC                                | Circle's bridged USDC standard                                       |
| [Native mint/burn gas token](/launch-arbitrum-chain/chain-config/costs/configure-native-mint-burn) | Your chain's gas token: a LayerZero OFT, an xERC20 token, or native USDC | Burn-and-mint through the token issuer's bridge        | The bridge provider deploys the adapter contract; the <Term id="chain-owner">chain owner</Term> allowlists it | ArbOS 41 or later, ArbOS 51 recommended; you enable it after genesis |
| [Third-party bridges](#third-party-bridges)                                                        | Tokens and messages, to chains beyond the parent chain                   | Depends on the provider                                | The bridge provider, by its own process                                                                       | Set by each provider                                                 |

<Callout type="warn" title="A third-party bridge adds trust">

If a third-party bridge mints your chain's gas token, your chain's liveness depends on that bridge. By default, the canonical bridge is the only contract that mints your gas token. See [Use native interop token with mint/burn as your Arbitrum chain gas token](/launch-arbitrum-chain/chain-config/costs/configure-native-mint-burn) for the security checklist.

</Callout>

## Third-party bridges

A third-party bridge does not work on your chain by default. Each provider decides whether and how it supports a new chain. Offchain Labs does not endorse or rank these providers. Contact each provider to learn its current process.

| Provider                                           | How it works                                                                                                                                         | How your chain gets support                                                                                                                                                                              |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [LayerZero](/third-party-docs/LayerZero/layerzero) | Messaging protocol. Omnichain fungible tokens (OFT) burn on one chain and mint on another.                                                           | LayerZero Labs deploys the LayerZero endpoint on a new chain. Contact [LayerZero](https://docs.layerzero.network/).                                                                                      |
| Hyperlane                                          | Messaging protocol. Warp Routes move tokens between chains.                                                                                          | Anyone can deploy the Hyperlane core contracts to an EVM chain with the Hyperlane CLI. See [Deploy to a new chain](https://docs.hyperlane.xyz/docs/guides/chains/deploy-hyperlane).                      |
| Wormhole                                           | Messaging protocol verified by a network of Guardians. Native Token Transfers (NTT) burn and mint, or lock and mint.                                 | The Wormhole Guardians run a node for every chain that Wormhole connects. See [Native Token Transfers](https://wormhole.com/docs/products/token-transfers/native-token-transfers/guides/deploy-to-evm/). |
| Across                                             | Intent-based bridge. Relayers fill a transfer on the destination chain, and Across repays them later.                                                | Across adds a chain after it deploys a spoke pool contract there. See its [new chain requirements](https://legacy-docs.across.to/resources/new-chain-requests).                                          |
| Everclear (formerly Connext)                       | Clearing layer. It nets the cross-chain rebalancing flows of solvers and settles them, including xERC20 transfers. It sends messages with Hyperlane. | See the [Everclear documentation](https://docs.everclear.org/).                                                                                                                                          |

xERC20 ([ERC-7281](https://github.com/connext/ERCs/blob/ERC-7281/ERCS/erc-7281.md)) is a token standard, not a bridge. The token issuer allows each bridge to mint and burn the token, and sets a rate limit for each bridge.

For more projects that support Arbitrum chains, see the [Arbitrum ecosystem portal](https://portal.arbitrum.io/chains/ecosystem).
