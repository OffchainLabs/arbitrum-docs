This page lists the changes to how <Term id="arbitrum-one">Arbitrum One</Term> orders, publishes, and accepts transactions, newest first. Each section after the list links to the pages that explain one topic.

## What changed

All dates are in UTC.

- **2026-10-28**: Offchain Labs announced changes to how `eth_sendRawTransaction` and `eth_sendRawTransactionSync` respond. The changes are not live yet. Read the [transaction submission notice](/notices/transaction-submission-changes-notice).
- **2026-09-23**: <Term id="priority-gas-auction">Priority Gas Auction (PGA)</Term> became the ordering policy on Arbitrum One.
- **2026-09-23**: The first <Term id="fast-feed">Fast Feed</Term> ticket round started at 17:00.
- **2026-09-23**: Offchain Labs turned off Timeboost on Arbitrum One. Until PGA became active the same day, the <Term id="sequencer">sequencer</Term> ordered transactions <Term id="first-come-first-serve-fcfs">first-come, first-served (FCFS)</Term>.
- **April 2025**: Timeboost started on Arbitrum One.

PGA replaces Timeboost on Arbitrum One only. Other Arbitrum chains can still use FCFS ordering.

## Transaction ordering

With PGA, you bid for an earlier position in the order: you attach a priority fee to your transaction. The sequencer orders transactions in rounds of 125ms.

## The Fast Feed

The Fast Feed is a paid WebSocket stream. It publishes each transaction after the sequencer orders and executes it, before the block reaches the standard <Term id="sequencer-feed">sequencer feed</Term>. The Fast Feed does not change the order of transactions.

## The sequencer feed and the feed relay

The sequencer feed is free and publishes each block. A Nitro node reads it to follow the chain. A <Term id="feed-relay">feed relay</Term> receives the sequencer feed and passes it on to your own nodes.
