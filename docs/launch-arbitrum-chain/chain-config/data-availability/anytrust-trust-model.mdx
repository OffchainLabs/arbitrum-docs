When you run an AnyTrust chain, you choose who holds your chain's data and how many of them must stay honest. This page covers four decisions and their consequences:

- Who sits on your <a data-quicklook-from="data-availability-committee-dac">Data Availability Committee</a> (DAC).
- How to choose `N` (committee size) and `H` (assumed-honest count).
- What happens when some or all members turn malicious.
- What you must be able to tell someone who audits your chain.

For how the protocol itself works, refer to [AnyTrust protocol](/how-arbitrum-works/deep-dives/anytrust-protocol.mdx).

## Who sits on your committee

Committee membership is permissioned, and you control it.

- You pick the members. No one joins your committee by bonding funds or by running software.
- Each member runs a <a data-quicklook-from="data-availability-server-das">Data Availability Server</a> (DAS) and holds a <a data-quicklook-from="bls-signature">BLS</a> key pair.
- A <a data-quicklook-from="keyset">keyset</a> lists every member's BLS public key along with `H`.
- The <a data-quicklook-from="chain-owner">chain owner</a> registers the keyset on the `SequencerInbox` contract by calling `setValidKeyset`. The contract rejects any <a data-quicklook-from="data-availability-certificate">Data Availability Certificate</a> (DACert) signed under a keyset it does not hold.

To change membership, generate a new keyset and register it. Refer to [rotate DAS keys](/launch-arbitrum-chain/operate/key-rotation.mdx) for the procedure.

One consequence deserves attention: the chain owner account is the real root of trust. Whoever controls that account can replace your entire committee in a single transaction. If the owner is one key held by one person, your committee's independence rests on that key rather than on the committee.

## Choosing `N` and `H`

A DACert needs `K` signatures, where `K = N + 1 - H`.

| `N` | `H` | `K` signatures per DACert | Members that can fail | Members that must stay honest |
| --- | --- | ------------------------- | --------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| 1   | 1   | 1                         | 0                     | 1                             |
| 3   | 2   | 2                         | 1                     | 2                             |
| 5   | 2   | 4                         | 1                     | 2                             |
| 5   | 3   | 3                         | 2                     | 3                             |
| 7   | 3   | 5                         | 2                     | 3                             |

`H` does two jobs at once:

- It sets how many members can go offline before the <a data-quicklook-from="batch-poster">batch poster</a> stops collecting enough signatures. That number is `H - 1`.
- It sets how many members must stay honest for the availability guarantee to hold. That number is `H`.

Raising `H` buys uptime and costs trust. Raising `N` while holding `H` at 2 does not improve uptime, because tolerance stays at `H - 1`. It does weaken the assumption you depend on, since two honest members out of seven is an easier bet than two out of three.

<VanillaAdmonition type="danger" title="The SDK default is a development scaffold">

`prepareNodeConfig()` from `@arbitrum/chain-sdk` generates `assumed-honest: 1` with a single backend. That is a one-member committee: no member can fail, and that one member holds all of your chain's data. The generated backend also points at `localhost` and carries a placeholder public key of all zeros. Use it for local development. Set `N` and `H` deliberately before you run a production chain.

</VanillaAdmonition>

Your keyset and your node configuration must agree on `H`. If they diverge, the aggregator can reject valid certificates or accept insufficient ones. Refer to [DAC and DAS operations](/launch-arbitrum-chain/chain-config/data-availability/dac-das-operations.mdx#dac-failure-modes-and-fallback-to-batch-posting) for the full failure-mode table.

## What happens when members turn malicious

Set `H` to 2 and work up the ladder.

| Malicious members | Honest members | 2-of-N assumption | Result                                                                                          |
| ----------------- | -------------- | ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Up to `N - 2`     | 2 or more      | Holds             | Every valid DACert carries at least one honest signature. That member serves the data.          |
| `N - 1`           | 1              | Broken            | The malicious members reach `K` without the honest one. They can certify data they never store. |
| `N`               | 0              | Broken            | Same as above.                                                                                  |

The `N - 1` row is the case worth understanding, because it is where the assumption fails rather than bends. With `H` set to 2 and only one honest member left, the malicious majority produces `K = N - 1` signatures among themselves. They sign a DACert for a data block, the batch poster posts it to the parent chain, and then they refuse to serve the data. The certificate is valid. The data is gone.

Here is the precise damage in that case:

- **They cannot forge transactions, mint tokens, or move funds.** Validity comes from executing the data, and validators prove the correct result on the parent chain. Withholding data does not let anyone prove a false result.
- **They can stop the chain.** Validators cannot reconstruct state from data they cannot read, so they cannot prove new assertions. The chain halts at the last provable point.
- **Confirmed assertions stay confirmed.** Work already settled on the parent chain does not unwind.

A committee that turns malicious costs you liveness. It does not let anyone steal from your users.

### Reduce your exposure

Two measures help, and both are yours to take rather than the committee's:

- **Run your own mirror DAS.** Anyone can run a server that syncs batch data and serves it over the REST API. A mirror you control means you can read your chain's history without asking the committee. Refer to [deploy a mirror DAS](/launch-arbitrum-chain/chain-config/data-availability/deploy-mirror-das.mdx).
- **Choose members that fail independently.** Five members in one data center under one operator give you `N` of 5 and the real resilience of 1. The protocol counts members; it does not check whether they are genuinely separate.

## What an auditor will ask you

If you run an AnyTrust chain in a regulated setting, expect questions about the committee. The protocol does not answer them for you. Your keyset, your member agreements, and your governance do.

<VanillaAdmonition type="warning" title="This is not legal or compliance advice">

AnyTrust is a technical design. Whether a given configuration meets a specific regulatory requirement is a question for your counsel. This section lists the technical facts you need before that conversation, not the conclusion of it.

</VanillaAdmonition>

| Question                                                 | Where your answer comes from                                                                                                                                       |
| -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Who are the committee members, as legal entities?        | Your keyset, plus the agreements you hold with each member                                                                                                         |
| Are the members independent of each other and of you?    | Your own governance. The protocol does not check independence.                                                                                                     |
| Which jurisdictions do the members operate in?           | Your member agreements                                                                                                                                             |
| How many members must stay honest?                       | `H` in your registered keyset                                                                                                                                      |
| How long do members retain data?                         | `node.batch-poster.anytrust-retention-period`, which defaults to `360h` (15 days). The batch poster requests this period; it is not a guarantee each member makes. |
| Who can change the committee, and how quickly?           | The chain owner account, by calling `setValidKeyset`                                                                                                               |
| What happens if the committee stops signing?             | The batch poster falls back to posting full data to the parent chain, unless you disable that fallback                                                             |
| Can you reconstruct chain history without the committee? | Only if you run an archive or mirror DAS                                                                                                                           |
| Which parties can read transaction data?                 | Every committee member, plus anyone who queries a REST endpoint                                                                                                    |

### Three points worth reiterating

1. **AnyTrust does not encrypt your data.** Committee members hold full <a data-quicklook-from="batch">batch</a> data, compressed but readable. The DAS REST API is designed to be public and cacheable. If your chain carries data that must not leave a jurisdiction or must not reach a third party, the committee is a party to that data, and AnyTrust alone does not protect it.

2. **Retention has a default and a floor.** The batch poster asks members to retain data for `360h` (15 days) by default, set by `node.batch-poster.anytrust-retention-period`. Separately, every node that reads a batch rejects a DACert that expires less than one week after the batch's maximum timestamp. Neither number is a permanent archive. If you must keep history, run an [archive DAS](/launch-arbitrum-chain/chain-config/data-availability/deploy-das.mdx#archive-da-servers).

3. **Committee independence is a governance property, not a protocol property.** The chain owner can swap the whole keyset. An auditor who accepts your committee today is accepting your owner-key governance along with it.
