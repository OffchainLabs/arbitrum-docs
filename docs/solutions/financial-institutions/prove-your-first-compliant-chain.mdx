## What you will prove

By the end, you will have a testnet AnyTrust chain that blocks a sanctioned address at the protocol level — the core of the [Financial institutions blueprint](/solutions/financial-institutions): a compliant, institution-operated chain you can take to a risk committee.

## Prerequisites

- The [Arbitrum chain quickstart prerequisites](/launch-arbitrum-chain/quickstart/sdk-introduction.mdx)
- A testnet parent chain and funded deployer account
- An S3-compatible bucket for the restricted-address list (see [Compliance Filtering configuration](/launch-arbitrum-chain/chain-config/validation/compliance-filtering.mdx))

## Step 1 — Deploy an AnyTrust testnet chain

Follow the [quickstart](/launch-arbitrum-chain/quickstart/l3-rollup-testnet.mdx), selecting the AnyTrust data-availability mode. _(Outline — expand with exact steps.)_

## Step 2 — Configure the Data Availability Committee

Stand up at least three DAC members. _(Outline — link [data availability configuration](/launch-arbitrum-chain/chain-config/data-availability/config-data-availability.mdx).)_

## Step 3 — Enable Compliance Filtering

Enable Sequencer- and STF-level filtering and point it at your restricted-address list. _(Outline — from [Compliance Filtering](/launch-arbitrum-chain/chain-config/validation/compliance-filtering.mdx); note the Delayed Inbox Sentinel must be activated at deployment.)_

## Step 4 — Verify a restricted transaction is blocked

Add a test address to the restricted list and confirm a transaction from it is rejected at the Sequencer, and that a force-included attempt fails at the STF. _(Outline — this is the "proof.")_
