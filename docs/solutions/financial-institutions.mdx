Bringing a regulated financial product onchain is a committee decision. Before a single contract ships, your product, engineering, security, and compliance leads all have to agree the architecture is sound — and your regulators have to be able to see it too.

This blueprint is for that group. It treats the move onchain as what it actually is: a stack of dependent decisions, not a menu of features. For a regulated institution the binding constraints aren't block times or benchmarks — they're **regulatory defensibility, operational ownership, and whether your own risk committee will sign off**. Get the order of these decisions wrong and you re-architect mid-flight, hand away chain economics you could have captured, or find you can't show a regulator that your controls match how the system actually runs.

So each decision below is framed around the objection your legal, compliance, and security reviewers will raise. The goal is a configuration you can take _through_ committee, not one that stalls in it.

**What this is:** an opinionated decision framework for the architecture of a compliant, institution-operated chain. **What it isn't:** a step-by-step deployment tutorial (those are linked under [Implement it](#implement-it)) or a sales pitch. It assumes you've decided to go onchain; the open questions are which controls live at the chain level, who operates it, and how you prove it's sound.

## The stack at a glance

Your architecture is five dependent layers. Each builds on the one below it, and two concerns — operations and cost — run through all of them. Read the stack from the bottom up; the sections that follow walk each layer as a decision.

## Decision 1 — Chain foundation

**The decision:** where your product runs, and on what trust model. First, a dedicated Arbitrum chain versus [Arbitrum One](/arbitrum-essentials/public-chains). Then, if dedicated, your data-availability model: a **Rollup** (post data to Ethereum) or an **AnyTrust** chain (a Data Availability Committee). This is the foundation everything else stacks on, and it sets your cost, your withdrawal options, and your decentralization posture.

| Your situation                                                                                                                                   | Recommended                    | Alternative     | Why                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------ | --------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A regulated product that needs chain-level control — embedded compliance, a custom gas token, dedicated throughput, ownership of chain economics | **A dedicated Arbitrum chain** | Arbitrum One    | Only a dedicated chain can run [Compliance Filtering](#decision-3--compliance--controls), set its own gas token, and capture fee economics. Arbitrum One can't.                                                  |
| A consumer product optimizing for fast, cheap UX at scale                                                                                        | **AnyTrust**                   | Rollup          | Lower cost, and it unlocks [Fast Withdrawals](#decision-4--interoperability--liquidity) with no new trust assumption. You accept a committee for data availability — structured with named, independent members. |
| Maximal trust-minimization or L2Beat standing is the priority                                                                                    | **Rollup**                     | AnyTrust        | Strongest trust-minimization, at the cost of higher fees and multi-day withdrawals.                                                                                                                              |
| A time-boxed pilot                                                                                                                               | **Start on Arbitrum One**      | Dedicated chain | Immediate liquidity and lowest overhead; migrate to a dedicated chain when your control needs grow.                                                                                                              |

**If you get it wrong:** choose a Rollup, then discover your consumer product needs 15-minute withdrawals, and you'll bolt a fast-confirmation committee onto it — quietly dropping the trust-minimization you chose Rollup for. Choose AnyTrust without structuring the committee as named, independent members, and your chain's trust rests on a black box a regulator will open first.

**The objection this answers:** _"Why do we need our own chain, which trust model, and what are we conceding on decentralization?"_

## Decision 2 — Key management and signing

**The decision:** how every privileged party on the chain is held and governed. On a dedicated AnyTrust chain with fast withdrawals, that set is larger than "who can move funds": the chain owner and upgrade authority, the sequencer, the validators, the Data Availability Committee, the fast-confirmation committee, and whoever can change the compliance rules.

| Your situation                        | Recommended                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Alternative                     | Why                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A production chain holding real value | **A small set of at least three independent, named operators**, each running a DAC node, a validator, and a fast-confirm signer, and sitting on the security-council multisig; signing via HSM/KMS or MPC; documented key rotation | Ad-hoc or single-signer control | One auditable, named trust set covers data availability, fast finality, governance, and compliance authority — so you can answer "who can act?" with a single, provable list |
| A testnet or early pilot              | **Managed keys, to move fast**                                                                                                                                                                                                     | A full council up front         | The overhead isn't justified yet — but commit to the migration path to institutional signing before mainnet value lands                                                      |

**If you get it wrong:** scatter these roles or leave them opaque, and you can't give a regulator one clean answer to _"who can move funds, upgrade the chain, sign for data availability, confirm withdrawals, and change the sanctions list?"_ Retrofitting governance across all of them after launch is slow and high-risk.

**The objection this answers:** _"Who can act on this chain — across funds, upgrades, data availability, fast confirmation, and compliance — and can we prove it?"_

Implementation: [ownership and access controls](/launch-arbitrum-chain/operate/ownership-and-access.mdx), [key rotation](/launch-arbitrum-chain/operate/key-rotation.mdx), [KMS signing services](/launch-arbitrum-chain/integrations/bp-kms-signing-services.mdx).

## Decision 3 — Compliance and controls

**The decision:** where you enforce regulatory controls — sanctions screening, KYC and permissioning, and reporting. Arbitrum ships a protocol-level capability for the hardest part.

| Your situation                                                     | Recommended                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      | Alternative                | Why                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| A regulated chain that must _prove_ sanctioned activity is blocked | **[Compliance Filtering](/launch-arbitrum-chain/chain-config/validation/compliance-filtering.mdx)** — screening in the Sequencer _and_ the State Transition Function — wired to a provider (TRM or Chainalysis), plus application-layer KYC/permissioning and your own reporting | Application-only screening | Only protocol-layer filtering, with the Transaction Guardian and Delayed Inbox Sentinel, closes the force-inclusion path — so _every_ route into your chain is screened, not just the ones your app controls |

Compliance Filtering enforces at two layers: the Sequencer rejects violating transactions before they're sequenced, and the State Transition Function catches anything force-included through the Delayed Inbox. Restricted addresses come from your provider as salted hashes, so the list is never stored in the clear.

:::note
Compliance Filtering is available in the ArbOS 61 upgrade but is **off by default**, and the recommendation is to adopt it at least 30 days after ArbOS 61 reaches Arbitrum One. The Delayed Inbox Sentinel must be **activated at chain deployment** — adding it later requires an ArbOS upgrade. Design for the parent-chain deposit edge case (ETH deposited by a restricted address can be stranded in the bridge), and note the burn exception that lets lending protocols liquidate restricted balances.
:::

Screening addresses is the shipped, protocol-level piece. **KYC, identity, permissioning, and regulator reporting remain application-layer or operational concerns.** Account-level transaction monitoring (KYT) is available through ZeroDev — see [Decision 5](#decision-5--accounts-and-wallets).

**If you get it wrong:** screen only at the application layer and a sanctioned actor can still transact through force-inclusion or a direct contract call — and you can't show a regulator that your controls match your operations. Discover the gap after launch and you're halting or re-architecting under scrutiny.

**The objection this answers:** _"Can we prove to our regulator and our own legal team that every transaction is screened and reportable?"_

## Decision 4 — Interoperability and liquidity

**The decision:** how value moves in and out — withdrawal finality, the bridging model, and launch liquidity.

| Your situation                                                             | Recommended                                                                                                                                                                            | Alternative                            | Why                                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A consumer product where multi-day exits are unacceptable                  | **[Fast Withdrawals](/launch-arbitrum-chain/chain-config/validation/fast-withdrawals.mdx) on an AnyTrust chain** — a committee of your DAC members confirms withdrawals in ~15 minutes | The standard ~6.4-day challenge window | On AnyTrust, fast confirmation reuses the trust you already place in the DAC, so it adds **no new trusted party**. On a pure Rollup, the same feature introduces a committee trust layer — less trust-minimized than you started |
| You need deep dollar liquidity and reliable UX at launch                   | **Native USDC (CCTP) over the canonical bridge**, with market-maker liquidity lined up for day one                                                                                     | Relying solely on third-party bridges  | Third-party bridge solver liquidity can run dry at launch, and users hit failed withdrawals                                                                                                                                      |
| Moving tokenized assets across your chain, Arbitrum One, and future chains | **A mint-and-burn / committee interop model**                                                                                                                                          | Lock-and-mint canonical bridging       | Mint-and-burn keeps a single canonical supply across chains                                                                                                                                                                      |

**If you get it wrong:** ship a consumer product on the standard challenge window and users can't exit for days; lean on third-party bridges and their liquidity dries up on launch day. Or flip on Fast Withdrawals on a _pure Rollup_ without grasping the tradeoff, and you've silently added a committee your decentralization story didn't account for.

**The objection this answers:** _"Can our users exit reliably and quickly — and what trust tradeoff are we accepting to get there?"_

Implementation: [Fast Withdrawals](/launch-arbitrum-chain/chain-config/validation/fast-withdrawals.mdx), [Delayed Inbox finality](/launch-arbitrum-chain/chain-config/sequencer/chain-finality.mdx), [bridged USDC](/launch-arbitrum-chain/integrations/bridged-usdc.mdx). A mint-and-burn interop model for tokenized assets is a custom build — talk to the Offchain Labs team.

## Decision 5 — Accounts and wallets

**The decision:** how your end users hold accounts and transact — smart accounts or raw externally-owned accounts — and how gas, onboarding, recovery, and controls work. This is the [ZeroDev](https://docs.zerodev.app) layer.

| Your situation                                               | Recommended                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                | Alternative                               | Why                                                                                                  |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Onboarding mainstream users at scale                         | **ZeroDev smart accounts** (ERC-4337 / EIP-7702) with [passkey/social login and recovery](https://docs.zerodev.app/sdk/advanced/recovery)                                                                                                                                                  | Raw EOAs and seed phrases                 | Mainstream users can't manage seed phrases; smart accounts give familiar login and account recovery  |
| Users shouldn't have to hold or understand gas               | **[Gas sponsorship](https://docs.zerodev.app/sdk/core-api/sponsor-gas)** and/or **[paying gas in a stablecoin](https://docs.zerodev.app/sdk/core-api/pay-gas-with-erc20s)**                                                                                                                | Making users acquire the native gas token | Removes the single biggest onboarding drop-off, and pairs with the gas token you chose in Decision 1 |
| The institution needs custodial or policy-controlled signing | ZeroDev with a **[custodial signer](https://docs.zerodev.app/sdk/signers/fireblocks)** (Fireblocks, Turnkey, Dfns) plus **[server-side gas policies](https://docs.zerodev.app/meta-infra/gas-policies)** and **[session-key permissions](https://docs.zerodev.app/sdk/permissions/intro)** | Client-only keys                          | Plugs institutional custody and programmable spending limits into the account layer                  |

**If you get it wrong:** ship raw EOAs and a native-gas requirement, and a mainstream product bleeds users at onboarding — and adding account abstraction later means migrating live users. No recovery means lockouts and a support burden.

:::info
Running ZeroDev on your own dedicated (Orbit) chain, and account-level transaction monitoring (KYT), are supported today but not yet covered in the public ZeroDev docs. **Talk to the ZeroDev team** to scope both for your chain.
:::

**The objection this answers:** _"How do millions of mainstream users transact without touching crypto or gas — and can we plug in our own custody and controls?"_

## Are you ready? {#are-you-ready}

Answer each question about your own system. Answer with a decision, not a _maybe_ — every "unsure" is a pre-launch work item for the owner named beside it.

**Foundation and data availability** — _product, engineering_

- Do your compliance, gas-token, throughput, or economic requirements need control at the chain level, or can they live in your application on shared infrastructure? _(Decision 1)_
- Does your cost and withdrawal-latency profile favor AnyTrust — and can you name and govern the DAC members a regulator will ask about? _(Decisions 1, 4)_

**Key management and governance** — _security, engineering_

- Can you name every privileged party — owner, sequencer, validators, DAC, fast-confirm committee, compliance admin — the threshold required to act, and how keys are stored and rotated? _(Decision 2)_

**Compliance and controls** — _compliance, legal_

- Can every transaction path, including force-inclusion and direct contract calls, be screened before it settles? _(Decision 3)_
- Can you produce the reporting your regulator requires, and who owns updating the restricted-address list? _(Decision 3)_

**Interoperability and finality** — _engineering, product_

- What withdrawal latency does your product require, and does your chain type achieve it without adding trust your risk committee hasn't approved? _(Decision 4)_
- Is launch liquidity provisioned so users can't hit dry-bridge failures? _(Decision 4)_

**Accounts and UX** — _product, engineering_

- Can users onboard, transact, and recover without managing keys or gas? _(Decision 5)_
- Does your account layer integrate your custody and controls? _(Decision 5)_

**Operations and cost** — _operations, finance_

- Who operates the chain, and who owns the 24/7 SLA, monitoring, and upgrades?
- Who funds gas sponsorship, and what are your gas-token and fee-capture economics?

## Recommended configuration {#recommended-configuration}

For a regulated institution bringing a consumer financial product onchain, this is the configuration we recommend — and the reasoning is the same one your committee will apply.

| Layer               | Recommended                                                                                                                                                            |
| ------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Foundation          | A dedicated Arbitrum chain, **AnyTrust** data availability, settling to Ethereum                                                                                       |
| Key management      | At least three named, independent operators running DAC, validator, and fast-confirm roles on a security-council multisig; HSM/KMS or MPC signing; documented rotation |
| Compliance          | Compliance Filtering (ArbOS 61) in the Sequencer and STF, wired to TRM or Chainalysis; application-layer KYC and permissioning; scheduled regulator reporting          |
| Interoperability    | Fast Withdrawals via the DAC committee (~15-minute finality, no new trust); native USDC over the canonical bridge; launch liquidity provisioned                        |
| Accounts            | ZeroDev smart accounts with gas sponsorship or stablecoin gas, session-key policies, and an optional custodial signer                                                  |
| Operations and cost | A 24/7 operated chain (by you or Offchain Labs); a stablecoin gas token; fee economics captured by the institution                                                     |

Why this configuration: every privileged party is named and governed, every transaction path is screened and reportable, users exit in about 15 minutes without a new trust assumption, and mainstream users onboard without touching gas — with the one real tradeoff, the AnyTrust committee, made explicit and structured as named, independent members.

**When it differs:** if maximal decentralization or L2Beat standing outranks cost and latency, choose a **Rollup** foundation and accept multi-day withdrawals. If you're running a limited pilot, **start on Arbitrum One** and migrate later.
